# Gift-Card Service — Requirements

Status: APPROVED by user 2026-08-14.

## 1. Summary & business goal

The web shop sells gift cards; shoppers redeem them at checkout, possibly
across several orders (partial redemption), until the balance is exhausted
or the card expires. This service owns issuance, redemption, balance
lookup, and expiry. The finance ledger (existing system) must learn of
every issuance and redemption for revenue recognition.

## 2. Locked decisions

- LD1: Gift cards expire **12 months after issuance**, to the day. No
  extension mechanism. (Source: business.)
- LD2: Card codes are **globally unique** and generated by this service:
  16 characters, uppercase A–Z and digits 2–9 (ambiguous 0/O/1/I
  excluded). A code is never reused, including codes of expired cards.
  (Source: business + fraud review.)
- LD3: **One service** — `gift-card-api` owns issuance, redemption,
  balance, and expiry. No split into separate issuance and redemption
  services: one small team, one entity family, no separately scaled hot
  path. (Source: tech review.)
- LD4: Partial redemption is allowed — a card is spent down across any
  number of orders. (Source: business.)

## 3. Actors & roles

| Actor | Goal |
|---|---|
| Shopper (via checkout) | Redeem a card against an order; check remaining balance |
| Commerce backend (existing, internal) | Request card issuance when a shopper buys a gift card; call redemption during checkout |
| Finance ledger (external) | Receive issuance and redemption events for revenue recognition |

## 4. Use cases

### UC1 — Issue a card
The commerce backend requests issuance with an amount. The service
generates a unique code (LD2); the card starts `active` with balance
equal to the initial amount and expires 12 months after issuance (LD1).
Acceptance criteria:
- AC1.1: The generated code is unique across all cards ever issued.
- AC1.2: The new card's balance equals its initial amount.

### UC2 — Redeem at checkout
The commerce backend redeems an amount from a card against an order.
Acceptance criteria:
- AC2.1: A redemption of amount ≤ current balance succeeds, decrements
  the balance, and is recorded with the order reference.
- AC2.2: A redemption exceeding the current balance is rejected; the
  balance is unchanged.
- AC2.3: A retry of the same order's redemption (same card, same order
  reference) is applied at most once — the retry returns the original
  outcome, never a double charge.
- AC2.4: Redeeming an expired card is rejected.
- AC2.5: A card whose balance reaches zero becomes `redeemed`.

### UC3 — Balance check
A shopper checks a card's remaining balance and expiry date by code.
Acceptance criteria:
- AC3.1: The response shows current balance, status, and expiry date.

### UC4 — Expiry
Active cards past their expiry date become `expired` and unredeemable.
Acceptance criteria:
- AC4.1: After its expiry date, a card is no longer redeemable (AC2.4)
  and its status reads `expired`.

## 5. Business rules

- R1: A code identifies exactly one card, forever — codes are globally
  unique and never reused (LD2).
- R2: A redemption never exceeds the card's current balance.
- R3: An expired card is not redeemable; expiry is 12 months after
  issuance (LD1).
- R4: Every redemption is recorded with the order reference that caused
  it; the same order reference is applied to a given card at most once.
- R5: The finance ledger is notified of every issuance and every
  redemption.

## 6. Integrations

| System | Direction | Data exchanged | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commerce backend | Inbound (commerce → gift-card-api) | issue requests (amount); redemption requests (code, amount, order reference); balance lookups | Synchronous calls during purchase and checkout. |
| Finance ledger | Outbound (gift-card-api → ledger) | issuance and redemption notifications (R5) | Delivery mechanism decided in architecture phase. |

## 7. Non-functionals

- NF1: Revenue-bearing and user-facing at checkout; target p99 redeem
  < 300ms. A missed ledger notification is recoverable via retry, not
  revenue-blocking.
- NF2: Traffic is **extremely spiky** — issuance and redemption
  concentrate around gift-giving holidays and flash sales, with
  near-zero overnight and off-season idle. Paying for standing capacity
  sized to the peaks is explicitly undesirable.
- NF3: Scale is modest — low tens of thousands of cards per year, peak
  redemption bursts in the tens of requests/second.

## 8. Environment context

- Cloud: Google Cloud Platform.
- Team: 3 backend engineers; existing services are Scala 3 + ZIO.
- Deployment platform: decided in the architecture phase.

## 9. Out of scope

- Card purchase/payment flow (the commerce backend owns payment).
- Refunds onto cards; reloading a card's balance.
- Physical/plastic gift cards.
- Multi-currency — the store operates in USD; all amounts are USD.
- Fraud scoring beyond the non-guessable code format (LD2).
